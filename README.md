# Quantum Computing Experiments

Computational experiments exploring the mathematics and concepts behind quantum computing, with a long-term focus on quantum algorithms and quantum cryptanalysis.

The experiments are developed while studying topics from textbooks, courses, papers, and other research material. The goal is not to reproduce study notes, but to turn theoretical concepts into computational investigations.

## Structure

```text
quantum-computing-experiments/
├── README.md
├── linear-algebra/
├── quantum-mechanics/
├── quantum-computing/
├── quantum-algorithms/
└── quantum-cryptanalysis/
```

The categories are intentionally broad. New experiments are added only when there is a meaningful question to investigate.

## Experiment Approach

Each experiment should follow a simple process:

1. Study the underlying theory.
2. Work through relevant mathematical exercises.
3. Formulate a question that can be investigated computationally.
4. Implement the experiment.
5. Observe and analyze the results.
6. Relate the observations back to the underlying mathematics.

The purpose is to move beyond simply implementing formulas and instead investigate how the concepts behave.

## Sources

The experiments may be based on concepts from:

* *Quantum Computing: From Linear Algebra to Physical Realizations* — Mikio Nakahara and Tetsuo Ohmi

The source of the relevant concept is identified within each experiment.

## Direction

The repository will gradually progress from mathematical foundations toward:

**Linear Algebra → Quantum Mechanics → Quantum Computing → Quantum Algorithms → Quantum Cryptanalysis**
